\section{Definitions and exact barrier}

Let \(B\) denote the true projected Euler bilinear form, and fix one
averaged bilinear operator \(\widetilde B_*\) for which Tao constructs
a smooth finite-time-blow-up evolution~\cite{Tao16}.

A premise \(P\) is \emph{operator-portable} when it has been proved for
both the \(B\) evolution and the \(\widetilde B_*\) evolution.  Its
constant may depend on \(\widetilde B_*\).

\begin{theorem}[operator-relative barrier]
\label{thm:barrier}
No valid global-regularity deduction for three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes can use only premises and inference steps portable to
\(\widetilde B_*\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose such a deduction existed.  Every premise would hold for the
\(\widetilde B_*\) evolution, and the portable inference steps would
give global continuation there.  This contradicts Tao's finite-time
blow-up solution.  Therefore a valid Navier--Stokes proof must use at
least one premise or inference which distinguishes the true
nonlinearity from \(\widetilde B_*\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem does not make portable estimates useless.  They may be
combined with a true-equation input.  Nor does it close a prescribed
q4 schedule: the known averaged blow-up need not enter that cell.
\end{remark}

\section{Realization logic}

The original obligation conflated two experiments.

\paragraph{Shell rung O-AVG-001a.}
A shell trajectory realizing the broad q4 profile proves only that the
explicitly imposed finite budget list is mutually compatible in that
model.  It is not a velocity field and cannot bind unformulated future
budgets.  Failure may reflect the model rather than a transferable
obstruction.

\paragraph{Averaged rung O-AVG-001b.}
A trajectory of \(\widetilde B_*\) in the q4 cell would establish a
genuine operator-relative limitation: an exclusion for true
Navier--Stokes must use nonshared structure.  Conversely, proving that
this averaged evolution avoids the cell does not by itself provide a
quantitative inequality which transfers to the true equation.

Thus neither experiment has ``both branches win'' logic.  Each remains
worthwhile only with its narrower success and kill criteria.
