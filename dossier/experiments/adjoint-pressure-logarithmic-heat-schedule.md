The linear-in-depth heat lookback used previously is more than the inherited
endpoint estimate needs.  There is no literal smallest summable schedule, but
the boundary order is logarithmic.  A near-borderline logarithmic schedule
keeps all lookbacks inside one terminal horizon, makes the inherited endpoint
summable, and changes the remaining terminal depth from
\((j+1)r_j^2\) to

\[
Q_jr_j^2,
\qquad
Q_j\asymp 1+\log(j+2).
\tag{1}
\]

Let

\[
\mu(s):=
\int_{T^*-s}^{T^*}\|\nabla v(t)\|_2^2\,dt.
\tag{2}
\]

If, for small \(s\),

\[
\mu(s)\le A\sqrt{s}\,\omega(s),
\qquad
\omega\ \text{nondecreasing},
\tag{3}
\]

then the whole adjoint-pressure upper audit is finite whenever

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}
Q_j^{1/4}
\omega\!\left(CQ_jr_\bullet^2L^{-2j}\right)^{1/2}
<\infty.
}
\tag{4}
\]

A genuine improvement over the critical square-root modulus has
\(\omega(s)\to0\) as \(s\downarrow0\); convergence of (4) already forces
decay along the scheduled nodes.

After normalising \(s\) by one fixed terminal time scale, fixed-ratio regular
monotone moduli obey the logarithmic Dini condition

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^{s_0}
\frac{\omega(s)^{1/2}
      [\log\log(e^e/s)]^{1/4}}{s}\,ds
<\infty.
}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, for \(\beta,\eta\ge0\), with

\[
\omega(s)
\lesssim
[\log(e/s)]^{-\beta}
[\log\log(e^e/s)]^{-\eta},
\tag{6}
\]

the bound (6) guarantees (4) precisely in the scalar-sharp range

\[
\boxed{
\beta>2,
\quad\text{or}\quad
\beta=2\ \text{and}\ \eta>\frac52.
}
\tag{7}
\]

One fixed scalar terminal history saturates this classification.  More
importantly, a disjoint-packet construction shows that the currently
available global
\(\nabla v\in L^{2+\delta_B}_{t,x}\), even together with uniform energy-norm
and weak-\(L^3\) bounds, cannot imply (4) by a norm inequality.  The
construction is not
a Navier--Stokes solution.  Thus the calculation classifies the gate but does
not close it.

## 1. A near-borderline summable heat schedule

Retain the notation of the two input theorems.  In particular,

\[
r_{n,k}:=\rho_nR_k,
\qquad
j:=K_n-k,
\qquad
r_{n,k}\le r_\bullet L^{-j},
\qquad
L\ge16.
\tag{8}
\]

The high-pass multiplier estimate in the staggered-ancestry proof supplies
fixed constants \(a_{\rm hp},C_{\rm hp}>0\) such that a lookback of
\(qR_k^2/\nu\) gives

\[
\left\|
\mathcal F^{-1}
\left(s_>(\eta)e^{-q|\eta|^2}\right)
\right\|_1
\le C_{\rm hp}e^{-a_{\rm hp}q}.
\tag{9}
\]

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and set

\[
q_j:=
\frac1{a_{\rm hp}}
\left[
\log(j+2)
+(1+\varepsilon)\log\log(e^e+j)
\right],
\qquad
\ell_{n,k}:=\frac{q_jR_k^2}{\nu}.
\tag{10}
\]

Then

\[
e^{-a_{\rm hp}q_j}
=
\frac1{(j+2)[\log(e^e+j)]^{1+\varepsilon}},
\tag{11}
\]

so the inherited endpoint series converges.  The physical lookback obeys

\[
\rho_n^2\ell_{n,k}
\le
\frac{r_\bullet^2}{\nu}q_jL^{-2j}.
\tag{12}
\]

The sequence \(q_jL^{-2j}\) is bounded, and

\[
\frac{\ell_{j+1}}{\ell_j}
=
\frac{q_{j+1}}{q_j}L^{-2}<1.
\tag{13}
\]

After shrinking the fixed \(r_\bullet\), (12) puts every lookback inside the
same smooth genealogy horizon and (13) keeps the physical intervals nested.
Every subsequent zero-data response, projected-work, signed-absorption, and
physical-pullback step in the two input theorems is unchanged.  Therefore

\[
P_n(T)
\le
C+C\widehat{\mathfrak D}^{\,\log}_n(T;r_\bullet)+o_n(1),
\tag{14}
\]

where

\[
\widehat{\mathfrak D}^{\,\log}_n
:=
\sum_{k=0}^{K_n}
\left(\frac{\widehat\delta^{\,\log}_{n,k}}{r_{n,k}}\right)^{1/2}
\tag{15}
\]

uses the extended intervals generated by (10).

This schedule has the smallest possible **order certified by** estimate (9).
Indeed,
if \(q_j=o(\log j)\), then eventually
\(e^{-a_{\rm hp}q_j}\ge j^{-1/2}\), so the heat-kernel majorant is not
summable.  This is not a lower bound on the actual inherited contribution.
There is no pointwise smallest summable choice: the borderline
\(\log j\) can be supplemented by successively slower iterated-log
corrections.  Equation (10) is a canonical near-borderline choice, not a
universal optimum for every possible use of the heat semigroup.

## 2. The discrete and continuous Dini criteria

The current window contributes \(O(r_{n,k}^2)\) to the physical pullback
depth.  Equations (10)--(13) therefore give

\[
T^*-\inf\widehat J^{\,\log}_{n,k}
\le
CQ_jr_{n,k}^2,
\qquad
Q_j:=1+q_j\asymp1+\log(j+2).
\tag{16}
\]

Assume (3), and shrink \(r_\bullet\) so every argument below lies in its
range.  Monotonicity of \(\mu\) and \(\omega\), followed by (8), gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left(
\frac{\widehat\delta^{\,\log}_{n,k}}{r_{n,k}}
\right)^{1/2}
&\le
\left[
\frac{\mu(CQ_jr_{n,k}^2)}{r_{n,k}}
\right]^{1/2}
\\
&\le
C_AQ_j^{1/4}
\omega(CQ_jr_{n,k}^2)^{1/2}
\\
&\le
C_AQ_j^{1/4}
\omega(CQ_jr_\bullet^2L^{-2j})^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]

Summing (17) proves (4), uniformly in \(n\), and then (14) bounds the
pressure audit.

The points

\[
s_j:=CQ_jr_\bullet^2L^{-2j}
\tag{18}
\]

are uniformly geometrically separated, while

\[
\log(e/s_j)\asymp j,
\qquad
\log\log(e^e/s_j)\asymp\log(j+2).
\tag{19}
\]

If \(\omega(cs)\asymp\omega(s)\) uniformly for \(c\) in each fixed compact
subinterval of \((0,1)\), then the series in (4) is equivalent, up to fixed
endpoint terms and constants, to (5).  This fixed-ratio regularity holds for
the logarithmic moduli below.  The discrete series (4) is the exact
sufficient condition used by the proof; (5) is its continuous Dini form.

## 3. Sharp logarithmic scalar histories

Write

\[
\Lambda(s):=\log(e/s),
\qquad
\Lambda_2(s):=\log\log(e^e/s).
\tag{20}
\]

Substituting (6) into the \(j\)-th term of (4) and using (19) gives

\[
Q_j^{1/4}\omega(s_j)^{1/2}
\asymp
j^{-\beta/2}
[\log(j+2)]^{1/4-\eta/2}.
\tag{21}
\]

The standard logarithmic series test proves (7).  At
\((\beta,\eta)=(2,5/2)\), (21) is comparable to
\(1/[j\log j]\) and diverges.  If a third factor
\(\Lambda_3(s)^{-\zeta}\) is inserted at that boundary, convergence holds
exactly when \(\zeta>2\).

This threshold is attained by one scalar history, not merely by a different
triangular array at every depth.  For sufficiently small \(s\), put

\[
F_{\beta,\eta}(s)
:=
c\sqrt{s}\,
\Lambda(s)^{-\beta}\Lambda_2(s)^{-\eta}.
\tag{22}
\]

For \(\beta,\eta\ge0\), \(F_{\beta,\eta}\) is increasing and absolutely
continuous near zero, with \(F_{\beta,\eta}(0)=0\).  Hence
\(g=F'_{\beta,\eta}\ge0\) is one finite scalar dissipation density and

\[
\int_0^s g(\tau)\,d\tau=F_{\beta,\eta}(s).
\tag{23}
\]

At the fixed nodes (18),

\[
\left(\frac{F_{\beta,\eta}(s_j)}{r_\bullet L^{-j}}\right)^{1/2}
\asymp
j^{-\beta/2}
[\log(j+2)]^{1/4-\eta/2}.
\tag{24}
\]

Thus this single history makes the action diverge exactly when
\(\beta<2\), or when \(\beta=2\) and \(\eta\le5/2\).  It is a sharp scalar
obstruction for the logarithmic schedule, not an NSE trajectory or a
regularity threshold.

## 4. Why Barker's global gain does not supply the modulus

Barker's theorem gives, on the terminal half-interval,

\[
\nabla v\in L^p(\mathbb R^3\times(T^*/2,T^*)),
\qquad
p=2+\delta_B,
\qquad
0<\delta_B<\frac12.
\tag{25}
\]

A direct temporal Hölder estimate for (2) would have to integrate over all
of \(\mathbb R^3\), whose measure is infinite.  This is not just a defect of
that proof attempt.  The following kinematic lemma rules out any implication
using only the displayed norms.

Fix a nonzero compactly supported smooth divergence-free field \(\phi\).
For \(N\) mutually disjoint translates, define

\[
U(x)
:=
A\sum_{m=1}^N
\phi\!\left(\frac{x-x_m}{R}\right).
\tag{26}
\]

Disjointness and the distribution-function definition of weak \(L^3\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U\|_2^2&=c_2NA^2R^3,\\
\|U\|_{L^{3,\infty}}&\le c_3ARN^{1/3},\\
\|\nabla U\|_2^2&=c_\nabla NA^2R,\\
\|\nabla U\|_p^p&=c_pNA^pR^{3-p}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

Normalise \(\|U\|_2=1\) and prescribe
\(\|\nabla U\|_2^2=G\).  Then \(R\asymp G^{-1/2}\), while

\[
\|U\|_{L^{3,\infty}}
\lesssim N^{-1/6}G^{1/4},
\qquad
\|\nabla U\|_p^p
\asymp
N^{1-p/2}G^{(5p-6)/4}.
\tag{28}
\]

For every \(p>2\), both right-hand sides can be made arbitrarily small at
fixed \(G\) by taking \(N\) large.

This can realise the one scalar history above.  Let \(s_j\) be (18), put

\[
d_j:=F_{\beta,\eta}(s_j)-F_{\beta,\eta}(s_{j+1}),
\tag{29}
\]

and choose a nonzero smooth temporal bump \(a_j\) supported in
\((T^*-s_j,T^*-s_{j+1})\), with \(0\le a_j\le1\).  Set

\[
G_j:=
\frac{d_j}{\int a_j(t)^2\,dt}
\tag{30}
\]

and choose \(N_j\) so large that the weak-\(L^3\) norm is uniformly bounded
and

\[
\int |a_j(t)|^p\|\nabla U_j\|_p^p\,dt\le2^{-j}.
\tag{31}
\]

The field \(V(t,x)=a_j(t)U_j(x)\) on the \(j\)-th disjoint time slab, and
zero elsewhere, is smooth and divergence free for every \(t<T^*\).  It
obeys

\[
\sup_{t<T^*}\|V(t)\|_2\le1,
\qquad
\sup_{t<T^*}\|V(t)\|_{L^{3,\infty}}<\infty,
\qquad
\nabla V\in L^p_{t,x},
\tag{32}
\]

but telescoping (29) gives

\[
\int_{T^*-s_J}^{T^*}\|\nabla V(t)\|_2^2\,dt
=F_{\beta,\eta}(s_J).
\tag{33}
\]

This field does not solve Navier--Stokes and need not satisfy its energy
inequality.  It proves only the exact functional point: the energy norm, global
weak-\(L^3\), and Barker-strength global gradient integrability do not force
the Dini modulus.  Any successful implication must use additional PDE
structure.

Even a hypothetical localisation of the coefficient dissipation to a
parabolic region of volume \(O(Q_jr_j^5)\) would give

\[
\left(\frac{\delta_j}{r_j}\right)^{1/2}
\lesssim
G_{p,j}^{1/p}
Q_j^{(1-2/p)/2}
r_j^{(2\delta-1)/(2+\delta)},
\qquad p=2+\delta.
\tag{34}
\]

Here \(G_{p,j}\) is the \(L^p\)-mass of \(\nabla v\) on that hypothetical
region.  The radius power is positive only for \(\delta>1/2\).  At
\(\delta=1/2\), a secondary-index or Dini estimate is still needed.
Barker's \(\delta_B<1/2\) remains on the wrong side even after this
hypothetical localisation; the actual projected pairing in the input
theorem is global.

## 5. Consequence for the live route

The lower-band decrement is a positive **lower** charge on each selected
flux interval.  Equation (4) requires an **upper** summability law for
nested terminal dissipation.  Without bounded time-frequency overlap,
freshness, or a scale-zero event floor, the same dissipation may be reused
and the charged fluxes may decay.  The logarithmic schedule does not reverse
that inequality.

This experiment therefore closes:

1. optimisation of the heat-lookback order for the inherited-endpoint
   estimate;
2. classification of the exact critical Dini series and its regular
   log--log threshold;
3. the earlier ambiguity about whether one fixed scalar history survives;
4. any norm-only deduction of that Dini gain from the published
   weak-\(L^3\) higher-integrability bundle; and
5. the same deduction after naive parabolic localisation when
   \(\delta_B<1/2\).

It does not prove (3) for an NSE trajectory, bounded overlap, fresh event
charges, an event-index contradiction, exclusion of the conditional ancient
profile, regularity, breakdown, or any Clay alternative A--D.

The remaining theorem on this branch must be genuinely NSE-specific: a
terminal global dissipation Dini estimate at least as strong as (4), a
localised critical estimate with its secondary index, or a
bounded-multiplicity/signed fresh-charge law coupling the lower-band
decrements to one finite trajectory budget.
