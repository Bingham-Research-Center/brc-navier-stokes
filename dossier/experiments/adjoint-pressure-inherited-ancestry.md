The reviewed tail-to-flux theorem left three possibilities at every
farther frequency \(K=R\Lambda_j\): comparable-annulus dissipation,
positive event-interval flux, or enough energy already present above
\(K\) at the entrance to the event.

Two elementary facts now collapse that trichotomy to signed flux.
First, on the common preterminal Navier--Stokes trajectory, the
entrance threshold cannot be accounted for solely by the tail present
at any fixed earlier time.  If

\[
T_j
=
\frac{c_\kappa}{C_\chi^2}
\sigma_jh_j^{-3},
\qquad
K_j
=
R_j\frac{\kappa h_j^{-1/2}}{\sigma_j},
\qquad
R_j>1,
\tag{1}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
T_jK_j^2
=
\frac{c_\kappa\kappa^2}{C_\chi^2}
R_j^2\sigma_j^{-1}h_j^{-4}
\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{2}
\]

At any fixed earlier smooth time \(t_\circ\), however,

\[
\boxed{
\|Q_{>K_j}v(t_\circ)\|_2^2
\le
K_j^{-2}\|\nabla v(t_\circ)\|_2^2.
}
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently, on every sufficiently late index where the inherited
branch gives

\[
\|Q_{>K_j}v(t_j^-)\|_2^2
\ge
\frac{\nu T_j}{2},
\tag{4}
\]

there is a last hitting time

\[
s_j\in(t_\circ,t_j^-),
\qquad
\|Q_{>K_j}v(s_j)\|_2^2
=
\frac{\nu T_j}{4},
\tag{5}
\]

and the exact sharp high-pass energy identity forces

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{K_j}\bigl((s_j,t_j^-)\bigr)
\ge
\frac{\nu T_j}{8}.
}
\tag{6}
\]

If inherited-branch indices are infinite, then
\(s_j\to T^*\) along precisely that index set.  Thus the inherited
bookkeeping branch is replaced by a terminal pre-event cumulative-flux
branch.

Second, the annulus can be squeezed away.  For each event, its finite
sharp Fourier-dissipation measure satisfies

\[
\int_{I_j}
\|\nabla
Q_{\Lambda_j<|\xi|\le R\Lambda_j}v\|_2^2\,dt
\longrightarrow0
\qquad(R\downarrow1).
\tag{7}
\]

Choose \(R_j>1\), with \(R_j\to1\), so that the quantity in (7) is
strictly below \(T_j/4\).  Reapplying the exact high-pass identity with
an entrance split at \(\nu T_j\) then gives the final one-way
conclusion:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
K_j:=R_j\Lambda_j,
\qquad
\frac{K_j}{\Lambda_j}\longrightarrow1,
\\
\textnormal{there is }J_j=(\alpha_j,\beta_j)
\textnormal{ with }
\alpha_j,\beta_j\longrightarrow T^*,
\\
\Phi_{K_j}(J_j)
\ge
\frac{\nu T_j}{4}.
\end{gathered}
}
\tag{7a}
\]

If the entrance tail is below \(\nu T_j\), take \(J_j=I_j\); otherwise,
take \(J_j\) from the corresponding last-hitting construction.  These
two cases are disjoint and exhaustive.  This is an existential,
event-adaptive cutoff
\(K_j/\Lambda_j\to1\); it does not assert the same flux floor at a
prescribed fixed factor \(R>1\).  The constant \(1/4\) is a clean
choice, not an optimisation.

This is a strict same-trajectory reduction, but not a regularity
theorem.  The intervals \(J_j\) may overlap, the same cumulative
cascade may pay many cutoffs, and no selected Besov event is yet
forced inside them.  The remaining problem is narrower: make these
terminal signed-flux payments event-index fresh, or prove that their
overlap and reuse are impossible.

## 1. Exact high-pass notation

Let \(v\) be a smooth finite-energy unforced Navier--Stokes solution on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(0,T^*)\), where \(T^*\) is the candidate first
singular time.  In the first-singular-time setting generated by smooth
rapidly decaying data, and explicitly for this note, assume

\[
v\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}\bigl((0,T^*);H^1(\mathbb R^3)\bigr).
\]

For \(K>0\), put

\[
Q_{>K}
:=
\mathbf 1_{\{|\xi|>K\}}(D),
\qquad
E_K(t)
:=
\|Q_{>K}v(t)\|_2^2.
\tag{8}
\]

For \(a<b<T^*\), retain the reviewed sign convention

\[
\Phi_K((a,b))
:=
-
\int_a^b
\left\langle
Q_{>K}\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(v\otimes v),
Q_{>K}v
\right\rangle\,dt.
\tag{9}
\]

Positive \(\Phi_K\) is nonlinear energy input into frequencies
\(>K\).  The classical sharp high-pass identity is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_K((a,b))
=
\frac12\bigl(E_K(b)-E_K(a)\bigr)
+
\nu
\int_a^b\|\nabla Q_{>K}v(t)\|_2^2\,dt.
}
\tag{10}
\]

All uses below occur strictly before \(T^*\), so no endpoint test on a
rough suitable solution is involved.

## 2. A general last-hitting ancestry lemma

### Lemma

Fix \(t_\circ\in(0,T^*)\).  Let

\[
a_j\longrightarrow T^*,
\qquad
K_j\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\Theta_j>0,
\qquad
\Theta_jK_j^2\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{11}
\]

Suppose

\[
E_{K_j}(a_j)\ge\Theta_j.
\tag{12}
\]

Then, for all sufficiently large \(j\), there is a last time
\(s_j\in(t_\circ,a_j)\) such that

\[
E_{K_j}(s_j)=\frac{\Theta_j}{2},
\tag{13}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{K_j}((s_j,a_j))
\ge
\frac{\Theta_j}{4}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

In addition,

\[
\boxed{s_j\longrightarrow T^*.}
\tag{15}
\]

### Proof

Plancherel gives, at the fixed smooth time \(t_\circ\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_{K_j}(t_\circ)
&=
\int_{|\xi|>K_j}
|\widehat v(\xi,t_\circ)|^2\,d\xi
\\
&\le
K_j^{-2}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}
|\xi|^2|\widehat v(\xi,t_\circ)|^2\,d\xi
\\
&=
K_j^{-2}\|\nabla v(t_\circ)\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

Because \(\Theta_jK_j^2\to\infty\), (16) implies

\[
E_{K_j}(t_\circ)<\frac{\Theta_j}{2}
\tag{17}
\]

for every sufficiently large \(j\).  The map
\(t\mapsto E_{K_j}(t)\) is continuous on
\([t_\circ,a_j]\).  Equations (12) and (17) therefore make the level
set

\[
\left\{
t\in[t_\circ,a_j]:
E_{K_j}(t)=\frac{\Theta_j}{2}
\right\}
\tag{18}
\]

nonempty and compact.  Let \(s_j\) be its maximum.  Since
\(E_{K_j}(a_j)\ge\Theta_j\), one has \(s_j<a_j\).  Applying (10) on
\((s_j,a_j)\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{K_j}((s_j,a_j))
&=
\frac12
\left(
E_{K_j}(a_j)-E_{K_j}(s_j)
\right)
+
\nu
\int_{s_j}^{a_j}
\|\nabla Q_{>K_j}v\|_2^2\,dt
\\
&\ge
\frac12
\left(
\Theta_j-\frac{\Theta_j}{2}
\right)
=
\frac{\Theta_j}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

It remains to locate the ancestor in time.  If (15) failed, a
subsequence and some \(\delta>0\) would satisfy
\(s_j\le T^*-\delta\).  Smoothness on the compact interval
\([t_\circ,T^*-\delta]\) gives

\[
M_\delta
:=
\sup_{t_\circ\le t\le T^*-\delta}
\|\nabla v(t)\|_2^2
<
\infty.
\tag{20}
\]

Using (13) and the same Plancherel bound at \(s_j\) would then give

\[
\frac{\Theta_jK_j^2}{2}
=
K_j^2E_{K_j}(s_j)
\le
\|\nabla v(s_j)\|_2^2
\le
M_\delta,
\tag{21}
\]

contradicting (11).  Hence \(s_j\to T^*\). \(\square\)

The use of one spatial derivative is important.  Mere
\(L^2\)-tail convergence at \(t_\circ\) would give
\(E_{K_j}(t_\circ)\to0\), but the inherited threshold
\(\Theta_j\) also tends to zero.  The weighted condition
\(\Theta_jK_j^2\to\infty\) supplies the decisive comparison.

## 3. Application to the parabolic pressure tail

Retain the selected smooth physical genealogy layers

\[
b_j(x,\tau)
=
\sigma_j
v(x_j+\sigma_jx,t_j-\sigma_j^2\tau),
\qquad
0<\tau<h_j,
\tag{22}
\]

with

\[
h_j\downarrow0,
\qquad
\sigma_j\downarrow0,
\qquad
I_j=(t_j^-,t_j^+)
=
(t_j-\sigma_j^2h_j,t_j),
\qquad
t_j^-\longrightarrow T^*.
\tag{23}
\]

For one fixed \(\kappa\ge1\), the reviewed coefficient-tail theorem
gives the physical smooth-tail floor whose sharp normalisation is

\[
T_j
=
\frac{c_\kappa}{C_\chi^2}
\sigma_jh_j^{-3},
\qquad
\Lambda_j
=
\frac{\kappa h_j^{-1/2}}{\sigma_j}.
\tag{24}
\]

Let \(R_j>1\) be arbitrary, including index-dependent, and set

\[
K_j:=R_j\Lambda_j,
\qquad
\Theta_j:=\frac{\nu T_j}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]

The only new scale calculation is exact:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_jK_j^2
&=
\frac{\nu}{2}
\frac{c_\kappa}{C_\chi^2}
\sigma_jh_j^{-3}
\left(
R_j
\frac{\kappa h_j^{-1/2}}{\sigma_j}
\right)^2
\\
&=
\frac{
\nu c_\kappa\kappa^2
}{
2C_\chi^2
}
R_j^2
\sigma_j^{-1}h_j^{-4}
\\
&\ge
\frac{
\nu c_\kappa\kappa^2
}{
2C_\chi^2
}
\sigma_j^{-1}h_j^{-4}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]

Thus the lemma applies whenever the inherited branch (4) occurs.  It
gives (5) and (6) at every sufficiently late inherited-branch index;
if those indices are infinite, it also gives \(s_j\to T^*\) along that
set.  Notice that this conclusion does not require cutoff-to-next-event
matching, the \(7/6\) scale ceiling, analytic smoothing, or a
quantitative lower bound on \(T_j\).  Indeed the reviewed global tail
continuity gives \(T_j\to0\); the frequency weight in (26) is what
overwhelms the fixed-time \(H^1\) tail.

## 4. Resolving the inherited bookkeeping branch

For any selected factors \(R_j>1\), the reviewed tail-to-flux theorem
says that at least one of

\[
D^\sharp_{\Lambda_j<|\xi|\le R_j\Lambda_j}(I_j)
\ge\frac{T_j}{2},
\tag{27}
\]

\[
E_{R_j\Lambda_j}(t_j^-)
\ge\frac{\nu T_j}{2},
\tag{28}
\]

or

\[
\Phi_{R_j\Lambda_j}(I_j)
\ge\frac{\nu T_j}{4}
\tag{29}
\]

holds.
If (28) holds at infinitely many indices, apply the lemma along that
index set.  It gives last-hitting times \(s_j\) satisfying (5), (6),
and

\[
s_j\longrightarrow T^*
\quad
\textnormal{along the inherited-branch indices}.
\tag{30}
\]

This is the exact scope of the ancestry statement.  Large entrance
energy may still occur.  The entrance threshold simply cannot be
accounted for solely by the tail present at any fixed earlier time:
the inherited-energy bookkeeping branch is replaced by a terminal
pre-event cumulative-flux branch.

## 5. Squeezing away the annular alternative

It remains to choose the farther factor rather than prescribe it.
For fixed \(j\), define a finite measure on frequency space by

\[
\mu_j(B)
:=
\int_{I_j}
\int_B
|\xi|^2
|\widehat v(\xi,t)|^2
\,d\xi\,dt.
\tag{31}
\]

Finiteness follows already from the sharp tail dissipation on the
smooth preterminal interval.  For \(R>1\), let

\[
A_j(R)
:=
\{\xi:\Lambda_j<|\xi|\le R\Lambda_j\}.
\tag{32}
\]

As \(R\downarrow1\), the sets \(A_j(R)\) decrease to the empty set.
Continuity from above of the finite measure therefore gives

\[
\mu_j(A_j(R))
=
D^\sharp_{\Lambda_j<|\xi|\le R\Lambda_j}(I_j)
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{33}
\]

There is consequently an \(R_j\) satisfying

\[
1<R_j<1+\frac1j,
\qquad
D^\sharp_{\Lambda_j<|\xi|\le R_j\Lambda_j}(I_j)
<
\frac{T_j}{4}.
\tag{34}
\]

Set \(K_j:=R_j\Lambda_j\).  The sharp total floor and the exact
orthogonal split give

\[
D^\sharp_{>K_j}(I_j)
>
\frac{3T_j}{4}.
\tag{35}
\]

If

\[
E_{K_j}(t_j^-)<\nu T_j,
\tag{36}
\]

then the exact identity (10), nonnegativity of the exit energy, and
(35) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{K_j}(I_j)
&=
\frac12
\left(
E_{K_j}(t_j^+)-E_{K_j}(t_j^-)
\right)
+
\nu D^\sharp_{>K_j}(I_j)
\\
&>
-\frac{\nu T_j}{2}
+
\frac{3\nu T_j}{4}
=
\frac{\nu T_j}{4}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{37}
\]

In this case define

\[
J_j:=I_j.
\tag{38}
\]

Otherwise,

\[
E_{K_j}(t_j^-)\ge\nu T_j.
\tag{39}
\]

Apply the general lemma with

\[
\Theta_j:=\nu T_j.
\tag{40}
\]

The product \(\Theta_jK_j^2\to\infty\) is twice (26).  Hence, for every
sufficiently large index satisfying (39), there is a last hitting

\[
E_{K_j}(s_j)=\frac{\nu T_j}{2}
\tag{41}
\]

with \(s_j\to T^*\) along any infinite set of such indices, and

\[
\Phi_{K_j}((s_j,t_j^-))
\ge\frac{\nu T_j}{4}.
\tag{42}
\]

In this case define

\[
J_j:=(s_j,t_j^-).
\tag{43}
\]

There are only finitely many indices before the last-hitting lemma
applies, and they are irrelevant to the asymptotic conclusion.  Since
\(R_j\to1\), (23), (37), (42), and (43) prove

\[
\frac{K_j}{\Lambda_j}\to1,
\qquad
\alpha_j,\beta_j\to T^*,
\qquad
\Phi_{K_j}(J_j)\ge\frac{\nu T_j}{4},
\tag{44}
\]

for every sufficiently large \(j\), where
\(K_j=R_j\Lambda_j\) and
\(J_j=(\alpha_j,\beta_j)\).  This is (7a).

The adaptive choice is qualitative.  It gives no uniform rate for
\(R_j-1\), and it does not claim a flux floor at any prescribed fixed
multiple of \(\Lambda_j\).  Its exact gain is existence of signed flux
across a boundary asymptotic to the reviewed parabolic cutoff.

## 6. What this advances and what it does not

The theorem closes one explicit item in the previous live list.  The
entrance threshold cannot be paid solely by a fixed earlier Fourier
tail; the last-hitting ancestors themselves converge to the candidate
singular time.  The adaptive sharp-annulus limit then removes the
annular bookkeeping branch as well.  Every sufficiently late
parabolic pressure-tail event has a terminal signed-flux interval at
some \(K_j/\Lambda_j\to1\).

It does **not** prove any of the following:

1. that the intervals \(J_j\) are disjoint;
2. that their flux floors are summable over \(j\);
3. that one cumulative cascade cannot cross many \(K_j\);
4. that \(s_j\) is a selected Besov regeneration event;
5. that the historical interval has a non-Zeno lower length;
6. a quantitative rate for \(R_j-1\);
7. the same conclusion at a prescribed fixed \(R>1\); or
8. regularity, breakdown, or any Clay alternative A--D.

The conservative shell ledger in the preceding theorem remains
relevant: it can reinterpret the newly identified historical input as
one earlier reservoir feeding many later boundaries.  The live
question is no longer “where did the inherited state come from?” but
“can the resulting terminal signed-flux ancestors overlap and reuse
one cascade without limit?”

The subsequent independently reviewed
[weak-\(L^3\) lower-band decrement theorem](adjoint-pressure-flux-decrement.md)
closes the asymptotically lossless version of that reuse.  Uniform
weak-\(L^3\) control splits every forced sharp flux into
viscosity-small far-low strain and a remainder containing a comparable
lower-band factor.  Every late event consequently dissipates at least
\(c\nu^2M^{-2}T_j\) in
\(\eta K_j\le|\xi|\le K_j\), for one fixed
\(\eta=\eta(\nu/M)>0\).  Cross-event band and interval overlap, and an
infinite geometrically decaying cascade, remain open.
