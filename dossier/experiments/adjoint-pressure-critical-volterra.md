# A critical scalar causal majorant need not be quasi-nilpotent

- **Experiment:** EXP-ADJOINT-PRESSURE-CRITICAL-VOLTERRA-001
- **Route:** ROUTE-R3B
- **Status:** analytic countermodel and conditional index reduction;
  [adversarially recomputed valid after two precision repairs](../review-ledger.md)
- **Domain:** \(\mathbb R^3\)
- **Clay status:** unsolved
- **Inputs:** the adversarially recomputed
  [logarithmic interaction-depth theorem](adjoint-pressure-divergent-interaction-depth.md)
  and Barker's published higher-integrability theorem
  `CLM-WEAK-L3-HIGHER-INTEGRABILITY-001`

The preceding theorem forces every selected feedback pressure packet beyond

\[
N(h)=\left\lfloor c_{\rm dep}\log\frac1h\right\rfloor
\tag{1}
\]

Stokes--Duhamel interactions.  The open question was whether causal time
ordering by itself makes the critical Oseen operator quasi-nilpotent.

This note closes that norm-only shortcut.  It proves three exact facts.

1. A scale-critical scalar causal operator has the constant function as an
   eigenvector with eigenvalue one, so causal ordering alone is not
   quasi-nilpotent.
2. Any fixed positive time-power gain changes the same model to a
   factorially summable operator.
3. Barker's published gain
   \(\nabla b\in L^{2+\delta}\) gives the standard Oseen heat-kernel route a
   positive time power only when \(\delta>1/2\).  The exponent constructed
   in Barker's proof is strictly below \(1/2\).

This is **not** a counterexample to Oseen or Navier--Stokes
quasi-nilpotence.  It shows that a proof must use structure absent from a
positive critical Volterra majorant, or obtain a regularity-strength
integrability gain.

## 1. Exact critical scalar countermodel

Fix \(0<\gamma<1\).  On \(L^\infty(0,T)\), define the positive causal
operator

\[
(\mathsf H_\gamma f)(t)
:=
\frac1{B(\gamma,1-\gamma)}
\int_0^t
(t-s)^{\gamma-1}s^{-\gamma}f(s)\,ds .
\tag{2}
\]

For every \(\lambda>\gamma-1\), the pointwise beta integral is finite
and gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf H_\gamma(t^\lambda)
=
\frac{B(\gamma,1-\gamma+\lambda)}
     {B(\gamma,1-\gamma)}
t^\lambda .
}
\tag{3}
\]

Within the declared \(L^\infty(0,T)\) operator domain, take
\(\lambda\ge0\).  All iterations below use only such exponents.
In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf H_\gamma 1=1,
\qquad
\mathsf H_\gamma^m1=1
\quad(m\ge1).
}
\tag{4}
\]

The kernel is positive and has mass one, so
\(\|\mathsf H_\gamma\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}=1\).  Equation (4) then
implies

\[
\boxed{
r(\mathsf H_\gamma)=1.
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus \(\mathsf H_\gamma\) is causal but not quasi-nilpotent.
Its differentiated-kernel exponent is \(1-\gamma\), its coefficient has
the critical time weight \(s^{-\gamma}\), and the two powers add to one.
The coefficient belongs to weak \(L^{1/\gamma}(0,T)\), not strong
\(L^{1/\gamma}\).  Accordingly, (2) is an exact scale-critical
**weak-endpoint scalar model**, not an Oseen realisation.

## 2. A positive time power changes the conclusion completely

For \(\varepsilon>0\), perturb (2) to

\[
(\mathsf H_{\gamma,\varepsilon}f)(t)
:=
\frac1{B(\gamma,1-\gamma)}
\int_0^t
(t-s)^{\gamma-1}s^{-\gamma+\varepsilon}f(s)\,ds .
\tag{6}
\]

Induction using (3) gives the exact formula

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf H_{\gamma,\varepsilon}^{\,m}1(t)
=
t^{m\varepsilon}
\prod_{j=1}^{m}
\frac{B(\gamma,1-\gamma+j\varepsilon)}
     {B(\gamma,1-\gamma)} .
}
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed, if the \(m\)-th input is \(t^{m\varepsilon}\), the next beta
parameter is \(1-\gamma+(m+1)\varepsilon\), and the output power is
\((m+1)\varepsilon\).

The individual ratio is

\[
\frac{B(\gamma,1-\gamma+j\varepsilon)}
     {B(\gamma,1-\gamma)}
=
\frac{\Gamma(1-\gamma+j\varepsilon)}
     {\Gamma(1+j\varepsilon)\Gamma(1-\gamma)}
\le C_{\gamma,\varepsilon}j^{-\gamma}.
\tag{8}
\]

The inequality follows for large \(j\) from the gamma-ratio asymptotic
and for the finitely many remaining \(j\) by enlarging the constant.
Since the operator is positive, (7)--(8) imply

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
\mathsf H_{\gamma,\varepsilon}^{\,m}
\right\|_{L^\infty(0,T)\to L^\infty(0,T)}
\le
\frac{(C_{\gamma,\varepsilon}T^\varepsilon)^m}
     {(m!)^\gamma}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}
\left\|\mathsf H_{\gamma,\varepsilon}^{\,m}\right\|
<\infty.
\tag{10}
\]

Equations (4) and (9) give the sharp qualitative divide: zero extra
time power permits an order-one survivor at every depth, whereas every
fixed positive time power gives factorial decay in this model.

## 3. The exact Oseen index generated by higher integrability

Let \(0\le\delta<1\), and suppose on a terminal interval \(I\) that

\[
\nabla b\in L^{2+\delta}(I\times\mathbb R^3).
\tag{11}
\]

Set

\[
p:=2+\delta,
\qquad
q:=\frac{3p}{3-p}
=\frac{3(2+\delta)}{1-\delta}.
\tag{12}
\]

For a finite-energy velocity with the inherited weak-\(L^3\) decay
normalisation, homogeneous Sobolev gives

\[
b\in L^p(I;L^q(\mathbb R^3)).
\tag{13}
\]

For

\[
(T_bz)(t)
:=
\int_0^t
e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}
\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(z\otimes b)(s)\,ds,
\tag{14}
\]

choose \(1<a<\infty\) with \(1/a+1/q<1\), and put
\(1/r=1/a+1/q\).  The differentiated Stokes heat-kernel estimate and
spatial Hölder give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}
\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(z\otimes b)(s)\|_{L^a}
&\le
C(\nu(t-s))^{-\vartheta}
\|z(s)\|_{L^a}\|b(s)\|_{L^q},
\\
\vartheta
&:=
\frac12+\frac3{2q}
=\frac3{2(2+\delta)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]

Time Hölder would therefore give

\[
\|T_bz\|_{L^\infty(0,t;L^a)}
\le
C\nu^{-\vartheta}
t^{\varepsilon_{\rm O}}
\|b\|_{L^p(0,t;L^q)}
\|z\|_{L^\infty(0,t;L^a)}
\tag{16}
\]

provided

\[
\vartheta p'<1.
\tag{17}
\]

The available time margin is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm O}
:=
1-\vartheta-\frac1p
=
\frac{2\delta-1}{2(2+\delta)}
=
\frac12
\left(1-\frac2p-\frac3q\right).
}
\tag{18}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm O}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\delta>\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac2p+\frac3q<1.
}
\tag{19}
\]

When (19) holds, (16) makes \(T_b\) quasi-nilpotent on
\(L^\infty(I;L^a)\).  To see this without assuming a pointwise time
profile, partition \(I\) into finitely many intervals \(I_k\) so short
that the right-hand operator norm of the diagonal restriction
\[
f\longmapsto
\int_{\inf I_k}^{t}
e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}
\mathbb P\operatorname{div}(f\otimes b)(s)\,ds
\]
is below an arbitrary \(\eta>0\).  Relative to this finite partition,
\(T_b\) is a causal triangular block operator.  Its spectral radius is
bounded by the largest diagonal-block spectral radius, hence by
\(\eta\).  Since \(\eta\) is arbitrary,

\[
r(T_b)=0.
\tag{20}
\]

This is an operator-level conclusion in \(L^\infty_tL^a_x\).  It does
not by itself prove summability in the global \(L^1\) pressure functional
of the selected branch.

## 4. Changing Lebesgue spaces cannot repair a negative margin

One might try to change the spatial exponent at every interaction.  Let
the input and output exponents at step \(m\) be
\(a_{m-1}\) and \(a_m\).  Whenever the heat estimate is admissible, its
kernel exponent and time margin are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\vartheta_m
&=
\frac12+\frac32
\left(
\frac1{a_{m-1}}+\frac1q-\frac1{a_m}
\right),
\\
\varepsilon_m
&=
1-\frac1p-\vartheta_m
=
\varepsilon_{\rm O}
-\frac32
\left(
\frac1{a_{m-1}}-\frac1{a_m}
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

The apparent flexibility telescopes:

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{m=1}^N\varepsilon_m
=
N\varepsilon_{\rm O}
-\frac32
\left(\frac1{a_0}-\frac1{a_N}\right).
}
\tag{22}
\]

Because \(0\le1/a_m\le1\), the second term is uniformly bounded.  If
\(\delta<1/2\), then \(\varepsilon_{\rm O}<0\), so the total margin tends
to \(-\infty\) linearly with \(N\).  No bounded Lebesgue-exponent
staircase can turn this particular heat-kernel/Hölder route into an
infinite-depth positive-gain argument.

The inherited \(L^\infty_tL^{3,\infty}_x\) endpoint does not help by
interpolation.  Interpolating it with (13) by a weight
\(0\le\lambda\le1\) gives

\[
\frac2{P_\lambda}+\frac3{Q_\lambda}
=
1+\lambda
\left(
\frac{3-\delta}{2+\delta}-1
\right).
\tag{23}
\]

For \(\delta<1/2\), this is strictly above one whenever
\(\lambda>0\).

## 5. Barker's exponent lies on the wrong side of the threshold

In the proof of Barker's Corollary 1, the nonlinear term and hence
\(\nabla b\) are placed in

\[
L^{2+\delta_B}(I\times\mathbb R^3),
\qquad
\boxed{
\delta_B
=
\frac{3C_{5,\rm univ}}
     {12M+6C_{5,\rm univ}}
=
\frac{C_{5,\rm univ}}
     {4M+2C_{5,\rm univ}}.
}
\tag{24}
\]

For every admissible sufficiently large \(M\),

\[
0<\delta_B<\frac12.
\tag{25}
\]

Algebraically, the displayed rational expression lies in
\((0,1/2)\) for every \(M>0\); the sufficiently-large qualifier is the
hypothesis under which Barker constructs it.

Substitution into (18) gives the exact negative margin

\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\rm O}(\delta_B)
=
-\frac{2M}{8M+5C_{5,\rm univ}}
<0.
}
\tag{26}
\]

Equivalently, the Sobolev pair has Serrin excess

\[
\boxed{
\frac2p+\frac3q-1
=
\frac{4M}{8M+5C_{5,\rm univ}}
>0.
}
\tag{27}
\]

The same half-integrability threshold appears in the packet ledger.
For a Navier--Stokes-scaled packet

\[
b_R(x,t)
=R^{-1}B(x/R,t/R^2),
\tag{28}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
\int|\nabla b_R|^{2+\delta}\,dx\,dt
=
R^{1-2\delta}
\int|\nabla B|^{2+\delta}\,dx\,dt.
}
\tag{29}
\]

Thus \(\delta<1/2\) makes arbitrarily fine packets cheaper, rather than
counting their depth.  At \(\delta=1/2\), both (18) and (29) become
scale-critical.

## 6. Why the missing half exponent is a major theorem

At the threshold \(\delta=1/2\), (12) becomes

\[
\boxed{
p=\frac52,
\qquad
q=15,
\qquad
\frac2p+\frac3q=1.
}
\tag{30}
\]

For the actual Navier--Stokes velocity, this is already the classical
Prodi--Serrin regularity line.  Therefore an upgrade from Barker's
\(2+O(M^{-1})\) gradient exponent to \(5/2\) would not be a minor
improvement of the present estimate: it would itself exclude the
putative first blow-up by a standard regularity criterion.

The valid conclusion is consequently narrow but decisive:

> The published higher-integrability gain does not supply a positive
> same-space Oseen time power, interpolation with weak \(L^3\) does not
> cross the Serrin line, and a varying Lebesgue-space staircase cannot
> repair the negative margin at arbitrarily large interaction depth.
> Critical causal ordering alone has an exact non-quasi-nilpotent scalar
> majorant.

This closes the proposed norm-only causal shortcut.  It does not exclude
an Oseen-specific cancellation.  The live question is now more precise:

> Does solenoidal projection, tensor sign, pressure cancellation, or
> actual same-trajectory ancestry force an infinite-depth improvement
> that no positive scalar majorant can see?
